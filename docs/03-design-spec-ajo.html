The circle, made visible.

Àjọ digitises esusu for immigrant civil servants in the UK. The visual language borrows from adire — Yoruba indigo resist-dye cloth: deep indigo grounds, undyed calico, brass accents. The interface behaves like a bank and feels like the circle: every screen answers "where is my money, and when do I get it?"

02 · Foundations

Colour — the adire system

Indigo grounds carry trust and identity; calico keeps long reading surfaces calm; brass is reserved for "your turn" and money-adjacent emphasis. Green and clay are semantic only — cleared and overdue — never decoration. All pairings meet 4.5:1.

03 · Components

States that never lie

Payment status pills

Paid · 18 Jul Due Friday 2 days late Reviewing

Late is stated as elapsed time — a fact — never "missed!!" or member-shaming copy. Vulnerable-member flags suppress the clay state pending human review (PRD E4).

Buttons

Pay contribution View circle ledger

Verbs say what happens: "Pay contribution", "Withdraw to my bank". Never "Submit". Money-moving buttons always trigger SCA step-up.

04 · Voice

Sound like the trusted elder, write like a bank

Money is literal

"Your funds are being processed 🎉"
"£2,000 sent to your wallet. Arrives by 2pm today."

Culture is respected, not costumed

"Welcome to the tribe!"
"Ẹ káàbọ̀, Amara — your circle is ready." Greetings localise (Yorùbá, Twi, Igbo, English); financial terms never do.

Honest about status

Silent spinner on partner outage
"Payments are delayed today. Your money is safe and nothing is lost — we'll confirm when it moves."
